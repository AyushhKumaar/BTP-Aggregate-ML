It can be seen from Fig. 11 that the single particle crushing strength

greater force is required to crush the particle [23]. Furthermore, it can 
be seen from Fig. 11 that using Eq. (8) to fit the relationship between 
single-particle sphericity and crushing strength results in a curve that 
fits well with the experimental results. The correlation coefficients R2 of 
the relationship curves between single-particle sphericity and crushing 
strength for RB, RC, and RM are 0.90, 0.99, and 0.98, respectively, 
indicating a significant effect of particle shape.

measurable through indoor experiments. However, it can be calculated

definition of work [36], as shown in Fig. 12. The crushing energy of a

Similar to the single-particle crushing strength, to further evaluate 
the reliability of the single-particle crushing energy data calculated from 
the test results, the box plot was used to statistically analyze the 9 sets 
(270 particles) of crushing energy results of the single particle of the 
recycled aggregate, as shown in Fig. 13. It can be seen from Fig. 13 that 
the overall distribution of particle crushing energy data is relatively 
uniform, and there is no abnormal value. The MAPE of each group of 
particle crushing energy data is calculated, which is within 1.08 %- 
10.1 %, and MAPE is within a reasonable range [17]. Besides, the par­
ticle crushing energy is not the same even if the particles with the same 
material properties. Therefore, the average single-particle crushing en­
ergy under the same particle shape (sphericity Φ) is used to replace the

It is evident from Fig. 14 that under the same particle sphericity Φ 
conditions, the single-particle crushing energy of RC is higher than that 
of RB and RM. When d=19–37.5 mm, the single-particle crushing en­
ergy of RC is 97.98 % and 64.22 % higher than that of RB and RM, 
respectively. This is mainly because the main components forming the 
RA are different. Generally, the strength of concrete blocks is higher 
than that of bricks and mortar, so even RC, RB, and RM particles of the 
same size and sphericity Φ contain more energy in RC particles, and 
thus, more energy is released during crushing. Additionally, it can also 
be found from Fig. 14 that under the same particle size, the closer the 
particle shape is to a sphere, the greater the crushing energy of a single 
RA particle. When Φ ranges from 0.4 to 0.6, the single-particle crushing 
energies of RB, RC, and RM are 349.05 mJ, 912.42 mJ, and 316.62 mJ, 
respectively. However, when Φ ranges from 0.8 to 1.0, the single-
