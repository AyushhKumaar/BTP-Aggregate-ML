It can be seen from Fig. 11 that the single particle crushing strength 
of RC is greater than that of RB and RM for particles of comparable 
sphericity, which is consistent with the conclusions obtained in Section 
4.2. It is found from Fig. 11 that as the particle sphericity increases, the 
crushing strength of RA also increases. When the sphericities of RB, RC, 
and RM are 0.531, 0.551, and 0.542, their crushing strengths are 
1.17 MPa, 2.74 MPa, and 1.12 MPa, respectively. However, when the 
particle sphericity increases to 0.856, 0.837, and 0.847, the crushing 
strengths of RB, RC, and RM increase to 2.88 MPa, 6.47 MPa, and 
3.20 MPa, respectively, representing increases of 146.15 %, 136.13 %, 
and 185.71 %. When particles have a comparable size, greater sphericity 
means the force is more evenly distributed across the particle, and a 
greater force is required to crush the particle [23]. Furthermore, it can
